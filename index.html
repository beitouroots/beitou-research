🏛️ Macro / The Fed

10-Year Treasury Yield Hits a Fresh 2007 High as a Hot Flash PMI Fans Fears the Fed Isn't Done Hiking

  • The tea: the 10-year Treasury yield is holding around 5.12%, its highest level since 2007, while the 30-year sits near 5.45%, extending Wednesday's surge on hot economic data. 📈
  • The trigger: S&P Global's September flash composite PMI jumped to 58.4 from 56.0 in August, the strongest reading since July 2021, with input costs rising at the fastest pace in nearly four years. 🧮
  • The Fed backdrop: the FOMC voted 12-0 to raise rates a quarter point to 3.75% to 4% on September 16, its first hike since 2023, and 16 of 18 officials now see at least one more hike before year-end. 🏛️
  • Why it matters: higher-for-longer yields raise the discount rate on every future dollar of earnings, which is exactly why growth and small-cap stocks are the first ones feeling it this morning. 🔍

5% Yields Are Now the AI Trade's Real Opponent, Not a Demand Slowdown

A risk-free 5%-plus return is now a real competitor to every long-duration growth story. 🏦

  • The counter: portfolio managers and Goldman Sachs both say AI demand signals, multiyear chip orders, inference growth, enterprise adoption, haven't cracked, and semiconductor capacity takes years to add, not quarters, so the fundamentals case for compute still stands. ⚖️

  • TL;DR: the next 6 to 12 months are a tug-of-war between a Fed that's hiking into 5% yields and an AI capex boom that still needs cheap capital to keep compounding, watch whether chipmakers can grow earnings fast enough to out-run the discount rate. 🔒
