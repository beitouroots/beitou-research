🧠 Deep Thoughts

Ray Dalio Says This AI Market Echoes 1929 and 2000, Evercore Says the S&P Still Has 16% Left to Run

  • The big idea: Ray Dalio says today's AI euphoria shows "classic signs" of a bubble, pointing to a Shiller CAPE ratio near 41, above the 32.6 level right before the 1929 crash and closing in on the 44.2 peak of the 2000 dot-com top. 💰
  • The bull case: Evercore ISI sees the S&P 500 climbing to 9,000, roughly 16% upside from here, arguing durable corporate earnings growth (the same earnings strength Jeremy Siegel is pointing to today) can keep justifying today's valuations. 📶
  • The bear case: Dalio's point isn't that a crash is imminent, it's that paper gains are ballooning faster than actual dollars flowing through the system, a setup where a sudden liquidity crunch can force fast, ugly selling regardless of how good the underlying technology is. 🧮
  • The wrinkle: both camps agree on the same input, AI infrastructure spending, they just disagree on whether today's prices already assume perfection; Nvidia's August 26 print will be an early test of which camp is closer to right. 🔍
  • TL;DR: keep riding the AI trade if you believe in it, but Dalio's real advice is boring and timeless: stay diversified, keep some cash on hand, and don't let a hot narrative talk you out of discipline. 🔒

  • The big idea: Monday was a clean tug-of-war between AI-capex optimism (Anthropic's reported $11.5B quarter, double-digit pops in memory and optical names) and geopolitical risk (fresh Iran/Oman threats, oil up over 2.5%, yields at multi-decade highs), and by the close the macro side won. 💰

  • The bear case: a 60-day US-Iran truce is expiring with no de-escalation in sight, oil's war premium is back, and rising long yields tighten financial conditions right as the market leans on a "soft landing" narrative that a real Hormuz disruption could puncture fast. 🧮
  • The wrinkle: this week's retail earnings, Home Depot tomorrow, then Target, Lowe's and Walmart, arrive with a live macro headwind attached, a tougher setup than a normal earnings week for reading the health of the American consumer. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the AI-capex story is still intact and arguably strengthening, but don't lose sight of the Middle East, it's the variable that actually moved the tape today, not anything in Silicon Valley. 🔒
