🌍 Geopolitics

US Hits Roughly 100 Iranian Targets Near the Strait of Hormuz, the Biggest Strike Since July

  • The tea: US Central Command carried out its largest strike since July 29, hitting roughly 100 IRGC targets in and around the Strait of Hormuz on September 1, including, for the first time, two Iranian government oil tankers, after Iran kept attacking commercial shipping that ignored its own transit rules. ⚔️
  • The retaliation: Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed missile and drone strikes on a US facility in Jordan and on Erbil, but Jordan's military said its air defenses intercepted 10 of 13 incoming ballistic missiles. 🛡️

  • The market read: oil rallied roughly 9% over three sessions before easing Thursday, WTI holding near $91.58 (up 0.63% pre-market) and Brent near $95.25, "both the US and Iran are looking for off-ramps here," per BOK Financial's Dennis Kissler, though "more peace talks could deflate prices quickly." 🛢️

⚖️ Regulation

A Federal Judge Spares Google's Ad Business From a Breakup, Orders Changes Instead

  • The tea: US District Judge Leonie Brinkema ruled that Google will not have to divest its AdX exchange or open-source its DFP ad-server auction logic, rejecting the Justice Department's push for a structural breakup of the ad-tech business a court found to be an illegal monopoly last year. ⚖️
  • The remedies: Brinkema said she is adopting most of the behavioral remedies both sides proposed, though the specifics stay under seal for 14 days while the parties file redactions, and both sides must submit a jointly proposed final judgment within 30 days. 📄
  • Why it matters: this closes out the biggest near-term overhang on Alphabet's ad business, a case that dates back to the DOJ's 2023 filing, with a meaningfully lighter outcome than the divestiture the government originally sought. 💰
  • The bigger picture: Google is still fighting a separate DOJ push to break up other parts of its business, so the regulatory story isn't over, just de-risked on this front. 🔍
Sources TechCrunch AdExchanger WTOP

🧠 Deep Thoughts

Earnings Reactions Are Getting Brutal, But the AI Capex Supercycle Isn't Blinking

  • The big idea: Broadcom's guide miss, Snowflake's faded pop, and Credo's 20% crater all say the same thing, the market has stopped rewarding AI-story stocks just for beating estimates, it wants acceleration or it sells first and asks questions later. 💰
  • The read: none of that has slowed the actual spending. Hyperscaler capex is on pace to exceed $800 billion in 2026, up roughly 67% year-over-year, and is forecast to cross $1 trillion in 2027, while the global semiconductor market is on track for $1.29 trillion this year. 🧮
  • The tension: leading-edge capacity is expected to stay tight into 2027 and possibly 2028, so the supply side of this trade is still years from catching up to demand, even as capital-spending growth itself decelerates from its current torrid pace. ⚖️
  • What's next: watch whether Thursday's other reporters extend the "good isn't good enough" pattern, and whether Friday's jobs report gives the Fed room to look past the Iran-driven inflation risk from Story 1. 🔌
  • TL;DR: the stock reactions are getting meaner, but the capex checks are still clearing. That gap between sentiment and spending is the trade to watch over the next 6 to 12 months. 🔒
