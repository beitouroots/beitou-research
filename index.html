Trump Claims "100% Control" of the Strait of Hormuz, Demands Iran Pay Reparations

🏛️ The Fed

September Hold Odds Jump to 60% as Traders Keep Digesting Friday's Ugly Jobs Miss

  • The tea: July payrolls fell by 23,000 against a call for +83,000, and May and June got revised down a combined 103,000 jobs, one of the uglier prints of the cycle. 📉
  • The whiplash: CME FedWatch odds of the Fed holding rates steady on September 16 have climbed to about 60%, up from 45% last Thursday and barely a coin flip the week before that, traders are pricing out a hike fast. 🔄

  • Why it matters: Chair Kevin Warsh already has three dissenters wanting a hike, a soft labor market gives the doves fresh ammo, but a hot CPI print tomorrow could flip the whole script (today's Big Brain has the full setup). ⚖️

🧠 The Big Brain · the 6-12 month bet
🧠 Deep Thoughts

The Fed Is Flying Without a Map Right as Wall Street Bets $500 Billion That AI Pays for Itself

  • The big idea: Chair Kevin Warsh dropped forward guidance from FOMC statements and launched five task forces to rebuild how the Fed communicates, a deliberate bet that less hand-holding makes for better policy. Goldman Sachs has publicly worried this approach could amplify confusion instead. Over the next 6-12 months, markets have to price the Fed's next move with less of a map than they're used to. 💰
  • The bull case: a labor market that's visibly cooling (a 23,000-job drop, 103,000 jobs of downward revisions, wage growth at a 5-year low) gives the Fed real cover to hold or eventually cut, and Nvidia's $500 billion financing platform means the AI buildout no longer depends solely on hyperscaler balance sheets, private capital is now underwriting the boom directly. 📶
  • The bear case: Hormuz-driven oil gains could show up in tomorrow's CPI and hand Warsh's three internal dissenters real ammunition to push for a hike, right as the Fed has voluntarily given up its clearest tool for talking markets down from a bad surprise. 🧮
  • The wrinkle: financializing AI infrastructure through institutional credit and insurance capital, instead of corporate cash, is a genuinely new structure. It can turbocharge the buildout, or it can quietly move AI-monetization risk off tech balance sheets and onto Wall Street's, exactly the kind of leverage nobody notices until it's a problem. 🔍
  • TL;DR: a Fed without forward guidance, a labor market that's cracking, and a half-trillion-dollar off-balance-sheet bet that AI pays for itself, that's the trade to watch over the next two quarters, not any single headline this week. 🔒
