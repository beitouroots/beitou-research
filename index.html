Bessent Launches "Operation Economic Outcast" to Cut Off Iran's Economy

  • The tea: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled a new secondary-sanctions campaign Monday branded "Operation Economic Outcast," aimed at severing Iran's remaining links to the global economy. 🚨
  • The targets: the sanctions hit Iran's oil, crypto, gold, aviation and shipping trade, blacklisting nearly 60 people, businesses and vessels, including a shadow-fleet network moving Iranian oil through the UAE, Hong Kong, China, Singapore and Switzerland. 🌐

  • The market read: oil fell anyway, WTI is down about 2% pre-market, traders look more focused on enforcement questions than on an immediate supply hit. 🛢️

🧠 Deep Thoughts

The "Debasement Trade" Is Back, Watch Whether Warsh Leans Into It or Against It

  • The big idea: Bitcoin just cleared $80,000 for the first time in three months and gold has rallied hard, and the trigger wasn't a crypto headline, it was the Treasury's plan to roughly double its bond buybacks to hold down yields that had been climbing toward multi-year highs. 💰
  • The read: when the government leans on buybacks to manage the price of its own debt while inflation has stayed above the Fed's 2% target for more than five years running, investors start pricing in currency debasement rather than just rate risk, that's the trade behind Bitcoin, gold and beta plays like MSTR all moving together. 🧮
  • The tension: Fed Chair Warsh built his reputation as an inflation hawk, but Friday is his first real chance to frame how he sees this fight, a speech that leans into fiscal-dominance concerns could pour gasoline on the debasement trade, one that pushes back could cool it fast. ⚖️
  • What's next: watch Wednesday's PCE print, Wednesday's Nvidia earnings, and Friday's Jackson Hole speech as a three-part test of whether this is a real shift in how markets price the dollar and rates, or just a summer trade that fades once the calendar clears. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the debasement trade is currently the loudest signal in markets, and the next 96 hours, PCE, Nvidia, Warsh, decide whether it's the start of something bigger or a summer flare-up. 🔒

Dick's Sporting Goods Just Had One of Its Worst Trading Days Ever

  • The tea: Dick's Sporting Goods shares crashed roughly 27% Tuesday, its steepest single-day drop in years, after second-quarter results and a full-year guidance cut badly missed Wall Street's expectations. 📉
  • The numbers: Q2 adjusted EPS of $3.53 came in below the roughly $3.76 to $3.78 analysts expected on revenue of $5.59 billion, and full-year guidance was lowered to about $21.9 to $22.2 billion in revenue with adjusted EPS around $11.50, well under the prior Street consensus. 🧾
