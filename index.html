🛢️ Geopolitics

Iran and Oman Say a Hormuz Deal Is in Its "Final Stage," But the Terms Look Like a Real Concession

  • The tea: Iran said Wednesday it's in the "final stage" of drafting a Strait of Hormuz agreement with Oman, with Trump floating an announcement landing any day, on a proposed 60-day interim arrangement. 🕊️
  • The catch: under the emerging terms, all inbound shipping into the Gulf would route through a northern lane in Iranian waters, giving Tehran a degree of shipping-lane control it didn't have before the war, while outbound traffic runs through Omani waters with no tolls for 60 days. 🚢
  • The history: a similar US-Iran framework collapsed three weeks ago when Iran resumed attacks on ships right after both sides thought they had a deal, so Washington is still weighing whether to accept these terms or risk another breakdown. 🔄
  • Why it matters: markets are pricing this as good news for oil supply, but the fine print, Iran gaining shipping-lane control, is exactly the kind of detail that could reignite the standoff (today's Big Brain). ⚖️

🏛️ The Fed

Rate-Hike Odds for September Just Jumped, and Oil Is the Reason Why

  • The tea: CME's FedWatch tool put the odds of a 25-basis-point Fed rate hike at the September meeting at 54.9% early Thursday, a sharp reversal from the Fed's earlier projection of one cut in 2026. 🏛️
  • The why: the Fed held its target range at 3.50%-3.75% on July 29 in an unusually split 9-3 vote, and Hormuz-linked oil spikes have kept inflation stickier than officials want, even as this week's deal hopes are pulling crude back down. 🔄
  • The other data point: Challenger, Gray & Christmas reported just 33,429 job cuts in July, the lowest monthly total in two years and down 46% from a year ago, so a still-solid labor market isn't the thing complicating the Fed's math. 📊
  • Why it matters: a Fed debating hikes instead of cuts, with oil swinging on Hormuz headlines by the day, is a genuinely unusual setup for August, worth watching into the September meeting. ⚖️

🧠 The Big Brain · the 6-12 month bet
🧠 Deep Thoughts

Oil, Iran and the Fed: Why the Next Six Months of Inflation Run Through the Strait of Hormuz

  • The big idea: for the first time in 2026, Fed-funds futures are pricing meaningful odds of a hike, not a cut, at the September meeting, and the swing factor is oil tied directly to the Hormuz standoff, not the usual jobs-and-CPI script. 💰
  • The bull case: if the emerging Hormuz deal holds and oil keeps drifting lower (WTI already off its highs near $75), disinflation returns and the Fed's original one-cut-in-2026 path stays intact, good for equities and rate-sensitive stocks alike. 📶
  • The bear case: this is the second time in three weeks a Hormuz deal has looked close, the last one collapsed within days, and the terms on the table (Iran gaining shipping-lane control) are politically fraught enough in Washington that a deal could unravel again and send oil, and inflation, right back up. 🧮
  • The next 6-12 months: watch the Hormuz deal's durability past its first 30 days (the naval mine-clearing deadline) as much as any CPI print, that's the tripwire that decides whether the Fed's next move is a cut or a hike. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the market has spent 2026 debating AI capex and earnings quality, but the variable that could move the Fed's hand next is a shipping lane half a world away. 🔒

🧠 Deep Thoughts

Even With a Peace Deal on the Table, Oil and Yields Went Up Today, Not Down

  • The takeaway: the market's been trading on a simple script all week, a Hormuz shipping deal should mean falling oil and falling yields, but today both climbed anyway, a sign traders aren't fully trusting the de-escalation narrative yet. 💰
  • The evidence: Iran and Oman's proposed shipping route explicitly does not amount to a full reopening of the strait, and fresh reports of tanker explosions and Houthi threats in the Red Sea kept a risk premium in crude even as headlines turned more optimistic. Meanwhile, Alphabet extending its slide on a $25 billion bond sale, and a run of guidance-driven software selloffs (AppLovin, Datadog, Celsius), show investors are demanding flawless execution and clean balance sheets, not just decent numbers. 🧮
  • The next 6-12 months: watch whether the Hormuz agreement survives past its first 30 days as closely as any Fed data point, since oil is now doing double duty as both an inflation input and a geopolitical risk gauge; also watch how many more software and AI-adjacent names get punished for guidance that's merely fine. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the market wants to believe the Middle East risk is fading and that AI spending keeps paying off, today it got a reminder that neither story is fully written yet. 🔒
