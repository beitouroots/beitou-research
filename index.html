An Anthropic Researcher Quit Over "Gambling With Our Lives." Trump Says He's Not Worried, China Keeps Building Anyway

  • The tea: Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon said this week he's quitting the company over concerns that Anthropic and rival OpenAI are "gambling with our lives," arguing that people building frontier AI increasingly believe it "could kill us all by the end of the decade," a view other researchers at the labs have echoed. 😟

  • Why it matters: the people closest to the technology are raising alarms at the exact moment the US, China, and Europe are all racing to pour more capital into building more of it, faster. 🔀

🧠 The Big Brain · the 6-12 month bet
🧠 Deep Thoughts

Yields Just Hit a 52-Week High and the AI Trade Ripped Anyway. Something Has to Give

  • The big idea: Friday closed with the entire Treasury curve at a fresh 52-week high, the 10-year near 4.96%, within reach of its highest since 2007, at the same moment Oracle's capex guidance sent Dell and HPE both roughly 11% higher this week and Sanmina jumped on its own AI-server beat. Rising real yields are supposed to eventually squeeze exactly this kind of capital-intensive growth story; this week, they haven't yet. 🏦
  • The read: Citigroup now forecasts Big Tech's cumulative AI infrastructure spending will cross $2.8 trillion by 2029, financed increasingly with debt, at the same time the market is pricing an 86% chance the Fed hikes rates again next Wednesday. Cheap money helped build the AI capex boom; the money is no longer cheap, and the boom hasn't paused to notice. 🧮
  • The counter: LPL's Jeffrey Roach argues AI investment and high-end consumer spending are now large enough, and interest-rate-insensitive enough, to keep nominal growth above 6% regardless of what the Fed does next week, essentially betting the AI buildout can out-earn its own financing costs. ⚖️
  • What's next: watch whether next Wednesday's Fed decision and any wobble in AI-linked hardware names arrive together. If data-center financing costs start to bite before AI revenue catches up, that's the correction thesis. If hardware demand keeps outrunning the yield story the way it did this week, that's the "the earnings are real" thesis. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the market is betting AI capex can keep compounding through the most expensive financing environment since 2007. That bet, not next Wednesday's 25 basis points, is the trade to watch over the next 6 to 12 months. 🔒

🧠 The Big Brain · the bottom line
🧠 Deep Thoughts

The Fed Now Has Its Excuse to Hike. The Question Is What It Does With It

  • The big idea: today's CPI print didn't shock anyone, but it did exactly what a hawkish Fed needed, it confirmed inflation is still running hot enough (3.4% headline, core steady at 2.4%) to justify a hike at next week's September 15-16 meeting, even with energy prices doing most of the lifting. 🏦
  • The read: gasoline, not services or shelter, drove more than a third of August's gain, a byproduct of the US-Iran standoff near the Gulf rather than domestic demand overheating. That's a genuinely awkward inflation print to hike into, tightening policy over a geopolitical oil shock, not a booming economy. 🧮
  • The tension: the stock market cheered both the in-line data and a very specific AI capex story, Oracle's guidance flowing into Dell, HPE, and Super Micro, that has nothing to do with rates at all. Two different markets are trading side by side, a rates market bracing for a hike and an equity market still chasing the AI buildout regardless. ⚖️
  • What's next: watch whether oil keeps cooling into next week's FOMC. A calmer Gulf plus this CPI print gives the Fed cover to hike once and signal it's done for a while, a fresh escalation reopens the whole debate. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the Fed got the number it needed to hike. Whether it stops at one is now a bet on the Strait of Hormuz, not on the domestic economy. 🔒
