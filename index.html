🛢️ Geopolitics

Pentagon Says the Iran Blockade Can Run "Indefinitely," While Hormuz Traffic Sits Near 3-Month Lows

  • The tea: Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said Thursday the US Navy can keep its blockade of Iranian ports going indefinitely, "because we'll rotate ships in and out, as we have, and we'll continue to." The blockade has run since mid-April, after peace talks in Islamabad broke down. 🚨
  • The dispute: Trump has said the US has "total control" of the Strait of Hormuz and might "keep it," but Iran's foreign minister called that a "miscalculation," insisting Tehran can still disrupt shipping. Traffic through the strait sank to a one-week low of about 8 ships a day, versus roughly 130 a day before the war began in February. 🌊
  • The market read: oil isn't reacting much anymore, WTI is roughly flat at $81.27 and Brent is up about 0.13% at $87.18 this morning, even as the IEA warns the world is burning through stockpiles the longer the strait stays effectively shut. 📉
  • Why it matters: Trump is under real pressure to end an unpopular war before November's midterms, so "indefinitely" cuts both ways, it signals resolve but also signals no resolution is close, and oil's flat premarket tape says traders have already priced in a long stalemate. ⚖️

🏛️ The Fed

Retail Sales Just Posted Their Worst Drop in Over a Year, and the Fed Bet Is Moving Again

  • The tea: July retail sales fell 0.6% to $763.6B, the sharpest monthly drop in more than a year and a big miss versus the roughly 0.1% rise economists expected, per Commerce Department data out this morning. 📅
  • The breakdown: motor vehicle and parts dealers led the slide, down 1.8%, nonstore (online) retailers fell 2.2%, gas stations dropped 0.9%, while apparel rose 1.9% and health and personal care gained 0.7%. Sales were still up 5.0% year over year. 🧊
  • The bond reaction: the 2-year Treasury yield dropped below 4.10%, its lowest since June 30, as traders further pared bets on a September hike, while the 10-year barely moved, sitting at 4.65%. 🔄
  • Why it matters: this is the third straight data point, after a weak jobs report and two cool-to-in-line inflation prints, chipping away at hawkish Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's case for a hike, and it lands hours before today's University of Michigan sentiment read, the next test of the American shopper. ⚖️

  • The big idea: on the same morning traders learned July retail sales fell the most in over a year, futures barely budged and stocks are still parked near records, a split between the "hard data" economy of consumer spending and the "AI capex" economy of chip equipment and memory that's been widening for weeks. 💰

  • The bear case: retail sales, jobs, and now consumer sentiment are all wobbling in the same direction, and the same "beat and still get punished" pattern that hit Cisco, Coherent, Cerebras and Tapestry this week, and now Applied Materials, is what a market with zero room for error looks like right before hard data catches up to it. 🧮

  • TL;DR: the AI capex economy and the Main Street economy are telling two different stories right now, and the next few weeks of retail earnings from Target and Walmart, plus Nvidia on August 26, will show which one the market actually has to listen to. 🔒
