Hormuz Optimism Curdles Overnight: Trump Says the US Is Only "Semi-Negotiating" With Iran

  • The tea: President Trump told Axios over the weekend the US is only "semi-negotiating" with Iran, while Iranian officials denied any direct talks are underway, a sharp cooldown from last week's hopeful tone. 🚨

🏛️ The Fed

Under New Chair Kevin Warsh, the Fed Just Watched September Hike Odds Get Cut in Half

  • The tea: the FOMC voted 9 to 3 on July 29 to hold the fed funds rate at 3.50% to 3.75%, Chair Kevin Warsh's second meeting since taking over from Jerome Powell in May, with three members dissenting in favor of a hike. 🏛️
  • The whiplash: Friday's surprise July payroll drop knocked CME FedWatch's odds of a September 16 hike down to about 44%, from roughly two-thirds a week earlier, traders now see a hold as the more likely outcome. 🔄
  • The next tell: July's CPI lands Wednesday morning, the first major inflation read since that split decision, and it's the number most likely to move both the hawks and doves on the committee. 📅
  • Why it matters: a Fed this divided means every data point this week, starting with CPI, carries outsized weight for where rates go next. ⚖️

🧠 Deep Thoughts

One Wednesday Morning Print Now Decides the New Fed Chair's First Real Test

  • The big idea: Kevin Warsh took over the Fed in May promising tighter inflation discipline, and his committee has already split publicly, 9 to 3 in July, with three members wanting a hike he didn't deliver. That is an unusually visible fight for a still-new chair. 💰
  • The bull case: if Wednesday's CPI comes in soft, the case for holding, or eventually cutting, gets easier. Friday's payroll miss already cut September hike odds to about 44% from roughly two-thirds a week ago, and a cool inflation read would cement that shift. 📶
  • The bear case: oil keeps climbing on Hormuz uncertainty, Trump himself says the US is only "semi-negotiating" with Iran, and if that risk premium shows up in Wednesday's number, Warsh's three internal dissenters gain real ammunition. 🧮
  • The AI wrinkle: none of this is slowing the AI capex story, TSMC's July revenue grew 45% and this week's CoreWeave, Rocket Lab and Applied Materials prints will show whether that holds, so the next 6-12 months is really a fight between a divided Fed and a still-roaring AI cycle. 🔍
  • TL;DR: forget Fed speeches this week, Wednesday's 8:30am CPI print is the single number most likely to move both rate odds and the market. 🔒

🔔 The Close

S&P 500 Closes at a Record as a Shockingly Weak Jobs Report Kills This Morning's Rate-Hike Talk

  • The scoreboard: the S&P 500 climbed 0.62% to a record close of 7,757.64, the Nasdaq Composite jumped 1.3% to 26,690.62, the Dow added 0.28% to 54,036.93, while the Russell 2000 was the odd one out, slipping 0.58%. 🟢
  • The trigger: the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported nonfarm payrolls fell by 23,000 in July, versus economists' roughly 80,000-job estimate, the first drop in months. The unemployment rate actually ticked down to 4.1% from 4.2%, but that was mostly a shrinking labor force, not more hiring. 📉
  • The whiplash: this morning's edition led with a Fed official pushing for a September rate hike. By this afternoon, a labor market that's visibly cracking (May and June payrolls were also revised down a combined 103,000) put that idea back on ice, Treasury yields fell and the dollar slid on the news. ⚖️
  • Why it matters: traders read "bad news" as "good news" for the Fed's next move, but a real payroll contraction, even one Wall Street is choosing to shrug off today, is worth watching, not celebrating blindly. 🔄

🧠 Deep Thoughts

One Jobs Report Just Flipped the Fed Conversation, and Software Just Took the AI Baton From Chips

  • The takeaway: this morning's edition led with a Fed official pushing for a September hike and chip stocks nursing a trillion-dollar drawdown. By the closing bell, a surprise payroll contraction had killed the hike talk and enterprise software, not chips, was carrying the AI trade. Both stories can be true within the same six-hour window, that's the whiplash of this market right now. 💰
  • The evidence: Take-Two, Atlassian, Twilio, Airbnb and Cloudflare all beat and raised today, a sharp reversal from yesterday's guidance-driven software selloff (AppLovin, Datadog, Celsius). Meanwhile The Trade Desk's 26.8% collapse and QuidelOrtho's guidance cut show this isn't a blanket "risk-on" rally, it's still a stock-picker's market where the outlook decides everything. 🧮
  • The next 6-12 months: watch whether July's weak payroll print is a one-off (government layoffs, seasonal noise) or the start of a real slowdown, that answer decides whether the Fed's September meeting is a hike, a hold, or a cut; also watch whether software's beat-and-raise streak holds up against chipmakers' upcoming reports. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the market spent this morning debating whether the Fed hikes rates, and this afternoon debating whether AI money is rotating from chips into software, both conversations are unresolved, and both restart Monday. 🔒
