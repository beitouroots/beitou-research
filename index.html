🏛️ The Fed

A Fed Official Just Said the Quiet Part Out Loud: Rate Hikes Are Back on the Table

  • The tea: St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem says inflation remains well above target with risks tilted higher, and that policy must impose "meaningful restraint" rather than stay easy to chase productivity gains, warning that doing otherwise risks the Fed's credibility. 🏛️
  • The context: Musalem has confirmed he favored a hike at the Fed's last meeting and argues gradual increases now beat abrupt ones later; he pegs underlying inflation at 2.5% to 3%, well above the Fed's 2% target, and calls financial conditions "very accommodative." 📈
  • The math: fed funds futures are pricing real odds of a quarter-point hike at the September 16 FOMC meeting, and JPMorgan Wealth Management strategists have shifted their base case to expect one, a sharp reversal from earlier 2026 expectations of a cut. 🔄
  • Why it matters: today's July jobs report is the last major data point before that meeting, more on that below. ⚖️

🧠 The Big Brain · the 6-12 month bet
🧠 Deep Thoughts

Rate Hikes Are Back in the Conversation, and Oil Out of the Middle East Is Why

  • The big idea: for most of 2026 the market debated when the Fed would cut. In a matter of weeks that flipped, fed funds futures are now pricing real odds of a hike at the September 16 meeting, and the swing factor is an oil-driven inflation scare tied to the Gulf, not the usual jobs-and-CPI script. 💰
  • The bull case: if Saudi Arabia's coordinated-attack warning proves preventable and the broader Gulf standoff cools, oil gives back its risk premium, inflation pressure eases, and the Fed can stay closer to its easier-policy path, good for stocks and rate-sensitive names alike. 📶
  • The bear case: Musalem's hawkish tone, inflation "well above target," policy needs "meaningful restraint," suggests at least some FOMC voters are already leaning toward acting regardless of how the Gulf situation resolves, and a real hit to energy infrastructure would only strengthen that camp's hand. 🧮
  • The AI wrinkle: even as the macro story wobbles, the underlying AI capex numbers, record semiconductor sales, Broadcom and Micron's blowout guides, keep validating the buildout, so the next 6-12 months is really a fight between a hawkish Fed and a still-roaring AI cycle for control of the market's direction. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the variable most likely to move the Fed's hand next isn't a spreadsheet, it's a shipping lane and a set of drone interceptions half a world away, watch Hormuz as closely as you watch the next Fed meeting. 🔒

🧠 Deep Thoughts

Even With a Peace Deal on the Table, Oil and Yields Went Up Today, Not Down

  • The takeaway: the market's been trading on a simple script all week, a Hormuz shipping deal should mean falling oil and falling yields, but today both climbed anyway, a sign traders aren't fully trusting the de-escalation narrative yet. 💰
  • The evidence: Iran and Oman's proposed shipping route explicitly does not amount to a full reopening of the strait, and fresh reports of tanker explosions and Houthi threats in the Red Sea kept a risk premium in crude even as headlines turned more optimistic. Meanwhile, Alphabet extending its slide on a $25 billion bond sale, and a run of guidance-driven software selloffs (AppLovin, Datadog, Celsius), show investors are demanding flawless execution and clean balance sheets, not just decent numbers. 🧮
  • The next 6-12 months: watch whether the Hormuz agreement survives past its first 30 days as closely as any Fed data point, since oil is now doing double duty as both an inflation input and a geopolitical risk gauge; also watch how many more software and AI-adjacent names get punished for guidance that's merely fine. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the market wants to believe the Middle East risk is fading and that AI spending keeps paying off, today it got a reminder that neither story is fully written yet. 🔒
