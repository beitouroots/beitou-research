🏛️ The Fed

The September Fed Meeting Is Now a Coin Flip, and the Coin Might Land on a Hike

  • The tea: CME FedWatch odds of a 25-basis-point hike at the September 15-16 FOMC meeting are running north of 58%, a sharp swing since Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech flipped a market that had leaned toward a hold. 🎙️
  • The dissent: three regional Fed presidents (Cleveland, Minneapolis, Dallas) already voted against July's hold, arguing rates needed to move higher then, not lower. 📈
  • The crosscurrent: July payrolls actually shrank by 23,000 with steep downward revisions to May and June, so the labor side argues for easing even as oil near $100 and inflation running above the Fed's 2% target for five and a half years argue for staying tight or hiking. ⚖️
  • Why it matters: the Trump administration, the President, the Vice President, and Treasury Secretary Bessent, is publicly pressuring the Fed not to hike, setting up a real independence showdown a week from today. 🗣️

🚛 Trade War

Canada's Retaliatory Tariffs Hit, and Trump Just Raised the Stakes With an Import Ban

  • The tea: Canada's retaliatory tariffs, ranging from 15% to 50%, on $27.6 billion of US goods took effect Tuesday after PM Mark Carney called US negotiating terms "unfair" and walked away from the table; Canada also doubled its steel and aluminum duties to 50% to match existing US tariffs. 🍁
  • The US response: Trump signed proclamations banning imports of Canadian alcohol, motorcycles, whey products, and molasses outright, plus 50% tariffs on a range of dairy, paper, wood, aluminum, and furniture products, all starting September 29. 🚫
  • The politics: Fortune notes the targeted goods read like a midterm hit list, Wisconsin cheese, Maine seafood, and Kentucky appliances among them, all in swing states. 🗳️
  • Why it matters: it's another cost-push item stacking on top of $100 oil right as the Fed weighs whether tighter policy can coexist with an escalating trade war. 🔀

🧠 The Big Brain · the 6-12 month bet
🧠 Deep Thoughts

The Fed Might Hike. The Treasury Is Already Easing. That's the Real 6-12 Month Trade

  • The big idea: while everyone watches whether the Fed hikes or holds on September 16, the bigger story may be the Treasury Department openly overriding bond-market stress on its own, announcing today it will buy back up to $6 billion of long-term debt, triple its normal operation size, after the 30-year yield hit 5.34% last week, its highest since 2007. 🏦
  • The read: national debt cleared $40 trillion in August, roughly two years ahead of the CBO's own 2028 forecast, and the Treasury is now buying back long bonds with one hand while funding it through more short-term bill issuance with the other, what economists call fiscal dominance, Treasury doing quasi-easing regardless of what the Fed decides. 🧮
  • The swagger: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told a crowd at Southern Methodist University this week, "I am the house now," daring currency traders to bet against the department's yen and Treasury market interventions. 🗣️
  • What's next: watch whether the buyback strategy actually holds long yields down through the September 15-16 FOMC, or whether a Fed leaning hawkish on oil-driven inflation collides with a Treasury that's loosening conditions from the other side. ⚖️
  • TL;DR: one arm of the government may be hiking rates next week while the other is actively easing financial conditions. That tension, not the up or down of one meeting, is the trade to watch over the next 6 to 12 months. 🔒

🧠 Deep Thoughts

The Treasury Tripled Its Buyback to Cap Yields Today. Yields Rose Anyway. That's the Real Story

  • The big idea: this morning's Buzz flagged the tension between a Fed that might hike next week and a Treasury quietly easing conditions on the other side. Today gave us the first real test, Treasury tripled its long-term debt buyback to $6 billion specifically to keep a lid on yields, and the 10-year yield rose to 4.857% anyway, its highest since November 2023. 🏦
  • The read: that's not nothing, it's a sign the bond market is more worried about $100 oil and a possible Fed hike than it is reassured by one buyback operation, however large. Fiscal dominance only works if the market believes it. Today it didn't, fully. 🧮
  • The tension: stocks that shipped real news, Meta's Muse launch, Marvell's guidance raise, Apple's first foldable, still moved on their own merits, while the index-level story stayed grim, a market that can separate stock-picking from macro-hedging even on a rough day. ⚖️
  • What's next: watch the 10-year yield and Brent crude heading into the September 15-16 FOMC meeting, if both keep climbing into next week, the coin-flip on a hike tips toward "yes" fast. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the government tried to talk yields down today and the market didn't listen. That's the tell to watch over the next week, not any single headline. 🔒
