🌍 Geopolitics

Bessent Declares "Economic D-Day" on Iran, and Oil Isn't Buying the Panic

  • The tea: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is unveiling a sweeping new wave of sanctions on Iran today, calling it in a Financial Times op-ed "an economic D-Day, the single greatest financial offensive ever marshalled against an adversary." 🚨
  • The scope: the measures aggressively expand restrictions on oil exports and the firms that move them, while also targeting new commercial sectors and financial networks, the goal is cutting Iran off from global commerce almost entirely. 🌐
  • The warning: Bessent told CNBC the US will "collapse" the Islamic Republic with the "toughest sanctions in history" and use its "full might" against any nation still doing business with Tehran. ⚔️
  • The twist: WTI and Brent both slid pre-market even with the sanctions landing today, oil traders look more focused on enforcement questions than on an immediate supply hit. 🛢️

🇨🇦 Trade War

US-Canada Trade Talks Collapse, 50% Tariffs Hit Canadian Goods

  • The tea: talks between Washington and Ottawa broke down Friday night after weeks of negotiations, and the US moved forward with 50% tariffs on more than $20 billion of Canadian goods (Carney's office puts the figure nearer $28 billion), everything from hockey sticks to building materials to wine to clothing. 🥅
  • The carve-outs: energy, potash and critical minerals were spared from the new duties, so Canada's biggest exports to the US stay untouched for now. ⛏️
  • The retaliation: PM Mark Carney called it "a miscalculation," suspended talks, and says Canada will match the tariffs dollar for dollar, concentrated on steel, dairy, appliances, farm equipment, pulp and paper and electronics, effective September 8. 🇨🇦
  • Why it matters: Carney blamed the collapse on last-minute US demands that would have cut EV tariff relief and restricted Canada's ability to strike other trade deals, this fight is about more than goods. ⚖️
Sources Axios NBC News PBS NewsHour

🖥️ Tech / AI

Nvidia's Big Week Kicks Off, and the "Circular Financing" Chatter Is Getting Louder

  • The tea: Nvidia reports fiscal Q2 earnings Wednesday after the close, its own guidance points to roughly $91 billion in revenue, and Wall Street is treating the print as a referee call on the entire AI trade. 🎮
  • The wrinkle: Nvidia is reportedly in talks to invest in Perplexity at a valuation north of $30 billion, up more than 50% from its last round, adding fuel to fintwit's "circular financing" complaint, that Nvidia's own customers are increasingly funded by Nvidia. 🔄
  • The bigger number: critics have been sounding alarms on Nvidia's roughly $750 billion in planned AI infrastructure spending, questioning whether the return math holds if any link in that chain wobbles. 💸
  • Why it matters: NVDA shares are sitting little changed pre-market, so the real move happens Wednesday, this week decides whether the AI trade gets a fresh leg up or its first real gut check. 🔍

🏛️ The Fed

Warsh's Jackson Hole Debut Looms Friday After the Fed's Most Divided Vote in Years

  • The tea: Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote Friday morning, August 28, under the symposium's theme "Financial Innovation: Implications for Payments and Policy," just 19 days before the September 16 rate decision. 🏔️
  • The backdrop: July's FOMC vote split 9-3 to hold rates at 3.5% to 3.75%, the most fractured vote in years, three regional bank presidents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) dissented in favor of a hike, not a cut. 🗳️
  • The style: Warsh, an inflation hawk by reputation, has skipped specific rate forecasts so far and says he wants to "frame the big questions" rather than preview a near-term move. 🎙️
  • Why it matters: the 10-year yield is sitting near a 20-month high around 4.72%, so any signal from Warsh Friday, hawkish or dovish, has room to move the whole curve. ⚖️

🧠 The Big Brain · the 6-12 month bet
🧠 Deep Thoughts

Circular Financing Is the New Tell, Watch Whether AI Capex Can Still Fund Itself

  • The big idea: Nvidia is reportedly circling a $30 billion-plus investment in Perplexity right as it heads into a Wednesday earnings print measured against roughly $750 billion in planned AI infrastructure spending, the same week Iran sanctions, a Canada trade war and the most divided Fed vote in years are all stacking up. 💰
  • The read: when the biggest supplier in an industry is also becoming one of its biggest funders, Nvidia and Perplexity today, Nvidia and OpenAI before it, the revenue on both sides of that ledger gets harder to tell apart from real end demand. 🧮
  • The tension: none of this breaks the AI trade on its own, capex funded by cash flow is very different from capex funded by circular deals, but the market has started asking the question out loud, and Wednesday's Nvidia print is the first real test of the answer. ⚖️
  • What's next: watch whether Wednesday's Nvidia numbers lean on hard cloud and enterprise demand or on related-party deals, and watch whether Warsh's Friday Jackson Hole debut adds a rate-hike scare on top of an AI-bubble scare, that combination is the real 6-to-12-month risk. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the AI trade doesn't need to be a bubble to correct, it just needs the market to stop taking "trust me" on faith, and Wednesday and Friday both test that trust in the same week. 🔒
