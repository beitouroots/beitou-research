🏛️ The Fed / Rates

The Fed Just Started a Hiking Cycle, the 10-Year Closed the Week Basically at 5%, and Stocks Shrugged

  • The tea: the Fed raised its benchmark rate 25 basis points to a range of 3.75% to 4.00% on Wednesday, its first hike in three years, with new chair Kevin Warsh warning inflation "remains elevated" and that the move should support "a timelier return" to the 2% goal. 🏛️
  • The bond reaction: the 10-year Treasury yield rose another 6 basis points Friday to settle at 4.995%, up nearly 27 basis points this month, while the 2-year closed at a fresh 52-week high of 4.741%; traders are now pricing roughly 55% odds of another hike by late October and about 90% odds of one by the Fed's December 9 meeting. 📈
  • The quote: JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon summed up the debate this week: "It's not clear to me we've slayed inflation." 🗣️
  • Why it matters: the Bank of Japan also hiked Friday to a 31-year high and the Bank of England flagged its own inflation risks, so this isn't just a Fed story, it's a synchronized global tightening turn, and the S&P 500 is still sitting only about 2% off its 2026 record high through all of it. 🌍
Sources CNBC Investrade / Hammerstone Alain Guillot

🛢️ Geopolitics / Oil

The Houthis Just Reignited Yemen's Civil War and Saudi Oil Exports Are Sitting in the Blast Radius

  • The tea: Iran-backed Houthi forces stormed down Yemen's Red Sea coast this week, seizing the port city of Mokha and islands near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, the chokepoint that roughly 12% of world trade passes through, reigniting a 12-year civil war that had gone mostly quiet. 🛢️
  • The pipeline risk: the offensive follows an attack on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline blamed on Iran-backed militias, and US diesel prices hit a record $6.29 a gallon this week, up 68% from a year ago, per Energy Information Administration data. ⛽
  • The market read: WTI still eased 1.6% Friday to $100.30 and Brent fell 0.9% to $103.87, crude's third straight down session, as traders bet the pipeline disruption won't hit supply as hard as first feared. 📉
  • Why it matters: roughly 125,000 Yemenis have been forced from their homes this week alone, and the fighting hands Iran fresh leverage right as the US has been trying to loosen Tehran's grip on the Strait of Hormuz on the other side of the peninsula. 🌐
Sources NPR Times of Israel Investrade / Hammerstone

SpaceX's Nasdaq-100 Weight More Than Doubles to 2.82%, and Index Funds Have to Buy Monday Whether They Like It or Not

🧠 The Big Brain · the 6-12 month bet
🧠 Deep Thoughts

The Market Just Re-Learned What a Hiking Cycle Feels Like, and AI Capex Is About to Meet a 5% Hurdle Rate

  • The big idea: the Fed hiked for the first time in three years, the 10-year closed the week basically at 5%, and the S&P 500 is still only about 2% off its 2026 record high. Nobody's panicking yet, but the premise behind the last few years, cheap money funding an enormous AI buildout, just got a lot more expensive. 🏦
  • The read: on The Compound and Friends, Josh Brown and Michael Batnick have spent recent episodes pressing guests on whether AI demand can actually justify the hyperscaler capex and debt boom. That question gets harder to answer at a 5% risk-free rate than it did at 4%, every dollar of data-center debt now competes with Treasuries paying investors to do nothing. 🧮
  • The counter: corporate earnings, consumer spending and the labor market are all still holding up, and per Lipper data, US equity funds have seen four straight weeks of outflows even as the index grinds near highs, meaning there's real sidelined cash that could still show up to buy any dip. ⚖️
  • What's next: watch whether the market keeps pricing an October or December hike as calmly as it did this week, or whether AI-linked names start wobbling first as financing costs climb faster than AI revenue does. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the AI trade got built over the last few years on the assumption that money stays cheap. Over the next 6 to 12 months, a 5% world is going to test whether the earnings are real enough to carry the debt that built it. 🔒

Warren Buffett Steps Down as Berkshire Chairman After Six Decades, Hands the Gavel to His Son

  • The tea: Warren Buffett, 96, is stepping down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway (BRK.A/BRK.B), the conglomerate he built since 1965, and becomes chairman emeritus effective immediately. He remains on the board. 👴
  • The succession: his son Howard Buffett becomes the new non-executive chairman, tasked mainly with preserving Berkshire's culture, while Greg Abel, who took over as CEO in a planned handoff last year, keeps making the day-to-day and capital-allocation calls. 🤝
  • The market's shrug: Berkshire shares barely budged on the news, the transition had been telegraphed since Abel's CEO appointment, and BRK.B is still lagging the S&P 500 by about 10 percentage points so far in 2026. 😐
  • Why it matters: "Father Time always wins," Buffett wrote in his letter to shareholders, closing the book on one of the longest and most consequential runs in corporate history. 📖

🧠 Deep Thoughts

The Fed Just Started Hiking Again, and Wall Street Partied Through It Anyway

  • The big idea: Wednesday's rate hike was the Fed's first in three years, and by Friday the 10-year Treasury yield had climbed to 5%, its highest level this cycle, not exactly the relief you'd expect once a hawkish move is priced in. 🏦
  • The tension: yields at 5% should be gravity for risk assets, yet crypto stocks just had one of their best weeks in months on a single SEC rule change, and the market barely blinked at Warren Buffett's succession, the biggest generational handoff in corporate America landing without a ripple. 🧮
  • The read: rates markets and equity markets are telling two different stories right now, one bracing for tighter policy to actually bite, the other still chasing the next deregulation-driven rally. That gap usually closes eventually, and rarely gently. ⚖️
  • What's next: watch whether the 10-year holds above 5% into next week's earnings from FedEx, Nike, and AutoZone, a level held that high for that long starts pressuring anything rate-sensitive, autos, homebuilders, regional banks, well before it shows up in the S&P headline number. 🔍
  • TL;DR: the Fed hiked, yields hit a fresh cycle high, and stocks shrugged. Somebody's wrong, and the next six to twelve months will settle who. 🔒
